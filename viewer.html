How the Flood Simulation Works

Bowling Green, KY · Warren County · DEM-based rainfall model

1. Why Bowling Green Floods Differently

Bowling Green sits on karst limestone terrain — a landscape riddled with underground caves, sinkholes, and internal drainage. When rain falls, water doesn't just run downhill toward streams: it also drains into sinkholes, collects in closed depressions, and can overwhelm underground conduits. This means low-lying areas and sinkhole basins flood before traditional riverside areas do.

The city also sits at the confluence of Barren River, Lost River (which disappears underground), and numerous unnamed karst tributaries — making flood prediction especially complex.

2. The Rainfall Slider (0" – 3")

The slider simulates a single storm event dropping between 0 and 3 inches of rain. The blue flood overlay expands as rainfall increases, showing which areas inundate at each threshold. The model is built from a 5-meter resolution DEM (KyFromAbove Phase 2 LiDAR) and uses elevation percentile thresholds:

3. Karst Sinkhole Boost

The purple Karst Sinkhole overlay shows 3,872 closed depressions identified from the contour data. Cells that fall within a significant depression are given a one-level early-flood boost — meaning they flood at lower rainfall than their raw elevation would suggest. This reflects the real-world behavior of karst drainage: sinkholes fill up before surrounding areas because they have no surface outlet.

4. FEMA Flood Zones

The red/orange/yellow FEMA overlay is the official National Flood Hazard Layer (NFHL), updated with 276 features across Warren County:

  • AE (red) — High risk; base flood elevation (BFE) established by detailed engineering study. Flood insurance is mandatory for federally backed mortgages.
  • A (orange) — High risk; no detailed BFE study. Similar requirements apply.
  • X (yellow) — Moderate or minimal risk; 0.2% annual chance flood zone or outside mapped areas.

5. Building Heights & Flood Risk Color

Buildings are rendered using OSM building footprints (20,284 structures) extruded to a uniform reference height and color-coded purely by flood risk tier. Tier is derived from the minimum rainfall (in inches) needed to inundate that building's ground elevation:

Buildings are placed at their actual ground altitude using the terrain layer — structures on hills appear elevated relative to those in valleys. Click any building to see its ground elevation above sea level and flood risk tier.

7. Data Sources

  • DEM: KyFromAbove Phase 2 LiDAR, 5m resolution, EPSG:32616
  • Parcels: Warren County PVA, all 40,987 parcels (including WKU, schools, government)
  • Buildings: OpenStreetMap via pyrosm; colored by flood risk tier
  • FEMA: NFHL via ArcGIS REST API, 276 features
  • Contours: 2-ft topographic contours, 660 MB source file
  • Sinkholes: Derived from closed depression contours (3,872 basins)
