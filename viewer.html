How the Flood Simulation Works

Bowling Green, KY · Warren County · GPU-accelerated hydrodynamic model · TACC Vista

1. Why Bowling Green Floods Differently

Bowling Green sits on karst limestone terrain — a landscape riddled with underground caves, sinkholes, and internal drainage. When rain falls, water doesn't just run downhill toward streams: it also drains into sinkholes, collects in closed depressions, and can overwhelm underground conduits.

The city also sits at the confluence of Barren River, Lost River (which disappears underground), and numerous unnamed karst tributaries — making flood prediction especially complex.

2. HPC Flood Simulation (TACC Vista)

The time-animated flood layers are produced by a 2D GPU flood solver implementing the Local Inertial Approximation (Bates et al., 2010) — the same numerical scheme used by LISFLOOD-FP. Run on TACC Vista's NVIDIA H100 Grace-Hopper nodes via CuPy, processing 80 million grid cells per timestep at 2-meter resolution across a 10×10 km domain.

3. The Rainfall Slider (0" – 3")

The slider simulates a single storm event dropping between 0 and 3 inches of rain. The blue flood overlay expands as rainfall increases, showing which areas inundate at each threshold based on terrain elevation percentile thresholds.

4. FEMA Flood Zones

The red/orange FEMA overlay is the official National Flood Hazard Layer (NFHL) for Warren County:

  • AE (red) — High risk; BFE established. Flood insurance mandatory for federally backed mortgages.
  • A (orange) — High risk; no detailed BFE study.
  • X (yellow) — Moderate/minimal risk; 0.2% annual chance flood zone.
